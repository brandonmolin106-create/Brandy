# 01 · Positioning (12 prompts)

Paste your brief (templates/00) first. Then one prompt at a time.

### 1.1 The one-liner, ten ways
```
Act as a launch copywriter who has written store pages for 200 indie products. Using the brief, write 10 one-line descriptions of {{PRODUCT}} (max 15 words each). Vary the angle: outcome, feeling, contrast with the usual option, the hook, the audience's words, a number, a dare, a question, a before/after, a plain statement. Rank them 1–10 by how likely a cold {{AUDIENCE}} is to click, and explain the top 3 in one sentence each.
```

### 1.2 Who actually buys this
```
Act as a product marketer. From the brief, describe three distinct buyer segments for {{PRODUCT}}. For each: a name, what they were doing five minutes before they found it, the sentence they'd say to a friend about it, the objection that stops them, and the one proof that answers the objection. Then pick the primary segment for launch and say why in two sentences.
```

### 1.3 The pain, in their words
```
Act as a researcher who reads forums for a living. For {{AUDIENCE}}, write 12 verbatim-style sentences they might post about {{PAIN}} (Reddit, Discord, X). Plain language, no marketing words. Mark the 3 that would make the best headline hooks and rewrite each as a headline under 10 words.
```

### 1.4 Hook test
```
Act as a sceptical editor. Here is the hook: "{{HOOK}}". Score it 1–10 on: surprising, specific, believable, demonstrable in a 6-second clip. For any score under 7, rewrite the hook to fix it. Give 5 alternative hooks drawn from the brief, each demonstrable in 6 seconds, and pick the strongest.
```

### 1.5 Differentiation table
```
Act as a competitive analyst. List the 5 options {{AUDIENCE}} already uses instead of {{PRODUCT}} (including "do nothing" and "free alternative"). Build a table: option, what it costs them (money and time), where it fails them, how {{PRODUCT}} is different in one concrete clause. Finish with one sentence that states the difference without naming competitors, for use on the store page.
```

### 1.6 Name check
```
Act as a naming consultant. Evaluate the name "{{PRODUCT}}" for: pronounceability, searchability (would it get lost among common words?), domain/handle likelihood, meaning to {{AUDIENCE}}, and collision with known brands. Score each 1–5. If total is under 18, propose 8 alternatives in the same spirit with a one-line rationale each. Do not propose names with licensed or trademarked words.
```

### 1.7 Tagline pairs
```
Act as a copywriter. Write 8 headline + sub-headline pairs for {{PRODUCT}}. Headline under 8 words, a promise. Sub-headline one sentence: who it's for and what changes. No adjectives that can't be proven by looking at the product. Mark which pair fits each platform in {{PLATFORMS}}.
```

### 1.8 Proof inventory
```
Act as a fact-checker. From the brief and anything I paste below, list every concrete, verifiable fact about {{PRODUCT}} (numbers, counts, durations, formats, compatibility, licence terms). Turn the strongest 6 into one-line proof points for a store page. Flag any claim in my draft copy that is not backed by a fact in the list.
```

### 1.9 The "why now"
```
Act as a strategist. Write three short "why now" angles for launching {{PRODUCT}} this month: one tied to a season, event or deadline the audience has; one tied to a shift in tools or platforms; one tied to the price or offer. Each under 40 words. Say which one to lead with on X and which on the store page.
```

### 1.10 Feature to benefit
```
Act as a copy editor. I'll paste a feature list. Rewrite each feature as a benefit sentence that starts with what the user gets, then the feature in parentheses. Cut any feature that doesn't change what the user can do. Return as a bullet list ordered by how much {{AUDIENCE}} cares.
```

### 1.11 Objection wall
```
Act as the most sceptical member of {{AUDIENCE}}. List the 10 objections you'd have before paying {{PRICE}} for {{PRODUCT}}. For each, write the one-sentence answer and name where it belongs: headline, proof point, FAQ, screenshot caption, or "fix the product".
```

### 1.12 Elevator pitch, three lengths
```
Act as a founder pitching at a meetup. Write the pitch for {{PRODUCT}} in three lengths: 10 words, 30 words, 90 words. Each must include the audience, the hook and the price. The 90-word version ends with the exact link text I should say out loud.
```
